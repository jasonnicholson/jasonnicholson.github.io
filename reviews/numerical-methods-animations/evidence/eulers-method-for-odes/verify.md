# Verification: eulers-method-for-odes

Reference check: I computed the true solution independently with mpmath `odefun` (30 digits) and scipy Radau (rtol 1e-12). Both give y(5) = 0.0081199204648680, which matches the script's RK4 h=1e-4 reference (0.008119920464867961). So the dashed curve is a valid stand-in for the true solution. I re-implemented both schemes in Python, h = 0.125 on 41 points, and the endpoints match the manifest's Julia run logs exactly (Euler -0.01744061866158267, Heun 0.2506349857845535).

## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: Heun's and Euler's values at every grid point against the high-precision solution, the linear stability factor h*J along the true solution (J = df/dy = -t/(3 y^{2/3})), the convergence of both methods as h is refined, and the committed final frame.
- Evidence: verify/check.py (output in verify/check_output.txt). At t=5: true 0.008120, Euler -0.017441 (error -0.0256), Heun 0.250635 (error +0.2425). Heun's endpoint error is about 9.5 times Euler's. h*J reaches -2 near t ≈ 3.7 (-2.12 at t=3.75, -2.60 at t=4, -5.16 at t=5). Both methods have the real-axis stability bound h*J ≥ -2, so both are unstable past t ≈ 3.7. Heun departs from the reference from t ≈ 4.1 on: 0.044 at 4.125, 0.140 at 4.25, then a steady climb to 0.25, while the true solution decays toward 0.008. In committed/eulersmethodbb/frame_041.png the magenta curve clearly ends at about 0.25, well above the dashed curve, which is near 0. Heun is far better than Euler on [0, 4] (errors ≤ 1e-2 against Euler's up to 8.6e-2), and it converges at second order once h is small enough (endpoint errors 2.9e-6, 3.8e-7, 7.6e-8, 1.7e-8, 4.2e-9 for n = 160 to 2560). So the failure comes from the step size the script uses, not from a bug in the method.
- Severity: fair as Error. The sentence's specific claim ("stays closer ... at the right endpoint") is false for the plotted data, and the GIF shows the opposite.
- Correction to the allegation: none. A minor addition: Heun does reduce drift over most of the interval, so the first half of the sentence ("reduces drift") holds on [0, ~4]. Only the endpoint claim is false.

## A2 — confirmed
- What I checked: the sign of the Euler error along the whole grid, and its behavior near t=5, against the high-precision solution. I also viewed committed/eulersmethodaa/frame_041.png.
- Evidence: verify/check.py. Euler's error is positive (Euler high) at every point for 0 < t ≤ 2.875, peaking at +0.086 near t=1.6–1.75. From t=3 to 4.25 the error is tiny (|err| ≤ 1.2e-2, mostly < 1e-3) and alternates in sign. From t ≈ 4.25 a growing sawtooth oscillation sets in: 0.0129, 0.0132, 0.0089, 0.0172, then -0.0071 at t=4.75 (negative), a spike to 0.232 at t=4.875 (true 0.0088), and finally -0.0174 at t=5 (true +0.0081). The frame shows the same picture: magenta clearly above the dashed curve on about [0.3, 2.9], then on top of it, then a visible spike near t=4.9 and a drop below zero at t=5. The final value is "low" only because the last step of a growing unstable oscillation happens to land low. The step before it was 26 times too high. This is not a smooth first-order drift. h*J < -2 for t > ~3.7 explains the instability.
- Severity: fair as Misleading. Read literally, "y_40 is below the reference" is true. But a reader would take away "Euler gradually drifts low because of O(h) error", when in fact Euler runs high and the end behavior is a stability failure (a spike, then a nonphysical negative value). One could argue for Error, since the stated mechanism is wrong, but Misleading is defensible.
- Correction to the allegation: a small precision point. "Runs high over most of the interval" holds for (0, 2.875], which is 57% of [0,5] and covers all of the large error. After that the error is small and alternates in sign until the oscillation grows. The values were also negative at t=4.75, not only at the endpoint.
