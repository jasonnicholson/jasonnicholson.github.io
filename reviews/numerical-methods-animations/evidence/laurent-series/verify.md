# Verification: laurent-series
## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked:
  1. Script `laurent_animations_all.jl`. `series_coeffs` (lines 119-223) and `series_coeffs_fit` (lines 68-117) return only n+1 coefficients for powers 0..n. The fit fallback, used for gamma (cases 22-24) and Y0 (cases 25-27), fits the basis `t^k, k in 0:n` (line 109). In `render_case` (lines 242-263) the drawn curve is `poly_eval_asc(coeffs[1:(n+1)], z)` (line 250), which is a degree-n polynomial in z = x - x0. `exps = -n:n` (line 243) is used only to build the legend string "Laurent orders $(first(exps))..$(last(exps))" (line 263). No negative-power term is ever computed, stored or evaluated. The script's own comment (lines 248-249) admits this. So every frame is an ordinary degree-N Taylor polynomial: exact Taylor coefficients for cases 01-21, and least-squares finite-difference estimates of them for cases 22-27.
  2. Committed frame `committed/laurent22/frame_005.png`: legend reads "Laurent orders -5..5" and the blue curve is a plain polynomial (Taylor about x0=1) that leaves Gamma near x≈2. It shows no 1/(x-x0)^k behaviour.
  3. Independent computation of the principal part for all 27 cases. I computed a_k = (1/2πi)∮ f(z)(z-x0)^{-k-1} dz for k = -1..-5 with mpmath at 30 digits, on a circle of radius 0.5 (no larger than half the distance to the nearest singularity or branch point) about each x0.
- Evidence: `verify/principal_part.py` → `verify/principal_part.out`. max|a_{-1..-5}| ≤ 9.0e-32 in every case (the size of quadrature round-off), and a_0 matches f(x0) to ≤1e-31. Every center is a regular point: sqrt/log/Y0 have their branch point at 0, Gamma has poles at 0,-1,..., tan has poles at ±π/2, 1/sqrt(1-x) and asin have branch points at ±1 or 1, atan has singularities at ±i, and none of these lie at x0. So none of the 27 cases has a non-zero principal part about its x0.
- Page wording: line 24 ("Each frame uses a two-sided truncation with both negative and positive powers at the same time") together with the Σ_{k=-N}^{N} formula (lines 26-28) and the GIF legends "Laurent orders -N..N" tell the reader that negative powers contribute to the drawn curves. They do not. With a_{-k}=0 the sum is formally the Taylor polynomial, so this is only "true" vacuously. Line 18 ("In many smooth cases centered away from singularities, the principal part is zero") is a true general statement. But on this page it suggests that some of the shown cases are not like that, when in fact all 27 are. Line 22 ("draws Laurent-series truncations instead of rational Padé forms") is technically true for the same vacuous reason. In practice the page shows Taylor polynomials for the Padé case set and never shows a principal part.
- Severity: Misleading is fair. The statements are defensible narrowly (a Laurent series with zero principal part is a Taylor series), but a reader would come away believing negative powers are drawn. You could argue the specific sentence "both negative and positive powers at the same time" (line 24) is closer to Error, since no negative-power term exists in any frame.
- Correction to the allegation: minor. The intro references are line 18 and lines 22-30, with the key wording at 24-30. The script lines 243-263 are correct. One nuance: for tan (case 12) and Gamma (cases 22-24), Laurent expansions about x0 on an annulus beyond the nearest pole do have non-zero principal parts. However, neither the script nor the page uses or mentions annuli, and the disk expansion the script uses has none. This does not weaken the allegation.
