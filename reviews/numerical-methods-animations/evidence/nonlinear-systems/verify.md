# Verification: nonlinear-systems
## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: Page wording at index.qmd lines 34-38 and the table row at line 65. Line 34 correctly gives the row-sum condition as a sufficient local condition ("converges provided ..."). But line 38 then says that when the inequalities are violated "the iteration typically diverges, regardless of how close the starting point is", and line 65 says the convergence guarantee "Requires row-sum condition". Mathematically, the local condition is that the spectral radius of the Jacobian of G at X* is below 1. The row-sum test checks the infinity norm, which is only an upper bound on the spectral radius, so it can fail while the iteration still converges from every nearby start.
- Evidence: verify/a1_check.py (run with `/usr/bin/python3 -I a1_check.py`).
  - Counterexample: G(x,y) = (0.6x + 0.8y + 0.1 sin y, 0.3y + 0.5y^2), fixed point (0,0). The Jacobian there is [[0.6, 0.9],[0, 0.3]], with row sums 1.5 and 0.3 (so the condition is violated) and eigenvalues 0.6 and 0.3. Starting from (0.1, 0.1) and from (0.001, 0.001), 200 iterations give about 1e-45 or smaller: the iteration converges.
  - Random 2x2 Jacobians with entries U(-1,1), 200k samples: 75% violate the row-sum condition, and 71% of those still have spectral radius < 1, so iteration converges locally. "Typically diverges" is therefore not even true in the typical case.
- Severity: Misleading is fair. Line 34 states sufficiency correctly, so the page is not wrong throughout. But line 38 says violation leads to divergence "regardless of how close the starting point is", which reads as a necessity claim, and the table repeats it ("Requires"). A reader would come away thinking the row-sum condition is necessary. Line 38 on its own is close to an outright false statement, because divergence from every nearby start needs spectral radius > 1, not just a failed row-sum test. Rating it Error for line 38 would also be defensible.
- Correction to the allegation (if any): None needed. Line numbers (38, 65) and quotes are accurate. One more point: line 68 ("requires careful formulation of G to satisfy the convergence condition") carries the same implication, but only mildly.
