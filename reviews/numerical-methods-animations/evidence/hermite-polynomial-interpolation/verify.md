# Verification: hermite-polynomial-interpolation
## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: Recomputed the max error of piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation of sin on [0, 2π] using scipy's CubicHermiteSpline (my own implementation, not the page's script). I used the script's 600-point evaluation grid and also a 200001-point dense grid, for n = 1, 2, 4, 8 (the frames in the GIF) and extended to n = 64 to see where the asymptotic rate kicks in. I also looked at all 4 committed frames.
- Evidence: verify/err_ratios.py -> verify/err_ratios.out. Max errors: n=1 0.4254, n=2 0.2146, n=4 0.01079, n=8 0.0009062 (same on both grids). Successive ratios over the frames shown: **1.98, 19.9, 11.9**. Beyond the animation: 15.0 (8->16), 15.7 (16->32), 15.9 (32->64). So the ratio approaches 16 only from n≈16 onward, and none of the three doublings the reader sees comes within 25% of 16. The first is about 2, which is nowhere near it. The committed frame titles agree with the computation ("max err ≈ 0.42", "0.21", "0.01…", "0.000…"). The titles are also clipped at the right edge of the 700-px frame, so frame 3 shows only "0.01" and frame 4 only "0.000" (actual 0.0108 and 0.000906). A reader can't even work out the n=4->8 ratio from the GIF, and what they can read (0.42 -> 0.21) is a factor of 2.
- Severity: Misleading is fair. O(h^4) convergence and the asymptotic factor of 16 are correct mathematics: the ratios do tend to 16, and the error at n=8 is close to the h^4/384 bound (0.00091 vs 0.00099). But the sentence sits directly above the animation and reads as describing it. Over n = 1, 2, 4, 8 the ratios are 2, 20, 12, so the reader would come away thinking the GIF shows a ×16 reduction per frame, which it does not. This isn't Error severity, because the claim about the convergence rate is true asymptotically.
- Correction to the allegation (if any): None needed. The line number (28) and the GIF are correct. Possible fixes: say "asymptotically (for small h)", or extend n_intervals_list to 16, 32, … so the ratios visibly approach 16. Separately from A1, the frame titles are clipped, which hides the error values.
