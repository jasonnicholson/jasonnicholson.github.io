# Verification: graeffes-method

## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: The page writes p(x) = a_n x^n + ... + a_0 (line 18, subscript = power), calls c_0..c_n "the coefficients of p_m" (line 32), and orders roots |r_1| >> ... >> |r_n| (line 28). Vieta for well-separated roots gives |r_1| ~ |a_{n-1}/a_n|, i.e. |r_k|^{2^m} ~ |c_{n-k}/c_{n-k+1}|. The page has the reciprocal, |c_{n-k+1}/c_{n-k}|. I evaluated the page formula literally on the exact (mpmath, 50 digits) Graeffe iterates and compared with the scripts' formula (|c[k+1]/c[k]|, highest-degree-first array), after checking the squaring step against numpy roots (step roots = squares of original roots).
- Evidence: verify/check.py. Case 1 (roots 4,3,2,1), m=6: page formula gives 0.25, 0.3333, 0.5, 1.0 (= 1/|r_k|); script formula gives 4, 3, 2, 1. Case 2, m=10: page 0.19986, 0.20014, 0.5, 1.0; script 5.0034, 4.9966, 2, 1. Under the other possible reading (c_0 = leading coefficient) the page formula at k=1 gives |c_n/c_{n-1}| -> 1 = |r_4|, so the values come out in reverse order against the ordering at line 28. Either way, the formula on lines 30 and 34 is wrong. The scripts' formula (lines 32-35 of graeffesaa.jl, 29-32 of graeffesab.jl) is correct.
- Severity: fair (Error). The formula as stated gives reciprocal magnitudes.
- Correction to the allegation: none. (The script comments write the correct ratio as |c_{n-k}/c_{n-k+1}|, which is the opposite of the page's version and confirms the inversion.)

## A2 — confirmed (minor precision)
- What I checked: I iterated the script's graeffe_step in IEEE float64 (same arithmetic as Julia Float64) and computed the script's estimates for m = 0..10. I also looked at committed frames 8, 9 and 11 of graeffesab.
- Evidence: verify/check.py, case 2. At m=7 all coefficients are finite (largest 2.9e217). At m=8 the coefficients are [1, -1.7e179, Inf, NaN, Inf], so the estimates are r1 = 5.01356 (valid), r2 = Inf, r3 = r4 = NaN. At m=9 and m=10 every estimate is NaN. Frames: frame_009 (m=8) adds only the r1 point (about 5.01). On frame_011 (m=10) the traces are the same as on frame_009, with no new points at m=9 or 10, yet the title says "m = 10" and the x-axis runs to 10. The convergence note added on the last frame describes linear convergence that the plot stops showing after m=7 (r2) and m=8 (r1). The high-precision values that would belong there exist: m=9 gives 5.0068/4.9932 and m=10 gives 5.0034/4.9966.
- Severity: fair (Misleading). The data shown are correct, but three of the 11 frames (m=8..10) quietly lose some or all points. The page text never mentions the overflow, even though line 42 describes this exact limitation in general terms.
- Correction to the allegation: "from about m = 8" is right, but frame 9 (m=8) still adds one valid point (r1). Only r2..r4 drop there. Frames 10 and 11 add nothing. Side note outside the allegation: Case 1 (graeffesaa.jl, n_iters=8) has the same overflow at its last frame. At m=8, r3 and r4 are NaN, and committed frame_009 shows those traces ending at m=7.
