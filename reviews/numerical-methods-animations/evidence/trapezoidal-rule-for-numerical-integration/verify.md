# Verification: trapezoidal-rule-for-numerical-integration
## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: (1) the wording of index.qmd line 29, and whether it applies to this case; (2) the convergence order the composite trapezoidal rule actually reaches for the animation's integrand f(x)=e^{-x}sin(8x^{2/3})+1 on [0,2], which is the same f, interval and T_n formula as trapezoidalaa.jl.
- Scope of line 29: it sits under "### Case 1 — Composite Trapezoidal Rule, f(x)=e^{-x}sin(8x^{2/3})+1 on [0,2]" as that case's "**Behavior:**" line. It says "the approximation converges toward the true integral with O(h^2) global error", so it is a claim about this integrand. Line 18 ("For smooth functions, ... O(h^2)") is the general statement and is correct as written, but this integrand is not smooth on [0,2]. Near x=0, f(x) ≈ 1 + 8x^{2/3}, so f'(x) ~ (16/3)x^{-1/3} is unbounded at the endpoint, and f'' is not integrable there. The usual O(h^2) error bound needs f'' bounded (the Euler–Maclaurin form needs f' at both ends), and that does not hold here. For an endpoint singularity like x^α, theory (Navot's extension of Euler–Maclaurin) gives a leading error of order h^{1+α} = h^{5/3}.
- Evidence: verify/order.py (mpmath, 40 digits), output in verify/order.out.
  - Reference integral I = 2.0162797196170963279 (computed with the substitution x=t^3, which removes the singularity; it agrees with a direct split-interval mp.quad to 1e-40).
  - Observed order log2(e_n/e_2n) for doubling n: 1.559 (n=16), 1.639 (32), 1.656 (64), 1.661 (128), 1.663 (256), 1.665 (1024), 1.6658 (8192), 1.6660 (16384). This tends to 5/3 ≈ 1.6667, not 2.
  - A least-squares slope of log|error| against log h over the animation's own n range (n=8..120) is 1.637.
  - Errors at the animation's n values: n=32: -1.19e-2, n=120: -1.34e-3. With O(h^2), going from n=32 to n=120 would cut the error by (120/32)^2 ≈ 14x. The actual reduction is 8.9x, which matches (120/32)^{5/3} ≈ 9.06.
  - The approximation does converge to I, so "converges toward the true integral" is right. Only the stated rate is wrong.
- Severity: Error is fair. Line 29 makes a specific quantitative claim about this case, and the claim is false: the measured and theoretical order is 5/3. Line 18 qualifies its claim with "for smooth functions", but line 29 applies the rate to an integrand that does not meet that condition, so a reader would wrongly conclude O(h^2) holds here.
- Correction to the allegation (if any): None needed. To be precise, the measured order is 5/3 (≈1.667), caused by the x^{2/3} endpoint singularity at x=0. The general statement on line 18 is not itself wrong.
