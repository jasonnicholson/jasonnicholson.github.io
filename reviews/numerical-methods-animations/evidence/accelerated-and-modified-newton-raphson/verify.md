# Verification: accelerated-and-modified-newton-raphson

## A1 — confirmed
- What I checked: The geometry of the segment drawn by accnewtonbb.jl line 49, `plot!([xn, xn1], [fn, 0.0])` with xn1 = xn - 2 f(xn)/f'(xn), compared with the tangent at xn. Algebraically, its slope is f(xn)/(xn - xn1) = f(xn)/(2 f(xn)/f'(xn)) = f'(xn)/2. That is exactly half the tangent slope at every step, so it is a line through (xn, f(xn)) with half the slope (equivalently, an "m-times-stretched" Newton step), not a tangent line. I also looked at the committed frame.
- Evidence: verify/check.py (mpmath, 30 digits). At x0=1.2: drawn slope 0.66, f'(x0)=1.32, ratio 0.5. The true tangent's x-intercept is 1.10303; the drawn line's is 1.00606. The ratio is 0.5 at every one of the 4 nonzero steps. committed/accnewtonbb/frame_002.png shows the orange segment from (1.2, 0.128) to (1.00606, 0) as a visibly shallower line that ends at the curve's bottom, not a line tangent at x=1.2. The script's own comment (line 48) says "Effective step … step is m× Newton", which is not a tangent.
- Severity: Misleading is fair. The page text on line 26 ("the line from (x_n, f(x_n)) to the x-axis intercept traces the next approximation") is literally true. But "Newton tangent-line diagram" (line 26, applied to all three cases) and "tangent-line animation" (alt text, line 44) tell the reader that the orange lines are tangents to f. They have half the tangent slope. Nothing on the page says this, and it is the key geometric point of Method A.
- Correction to the allegation: none of substance. Line 26 calls all three animations tangent-line diagrams; the Case 2 claim is the false part. (Case 3 lines are true tangents of h, which I did not check in depth because it is out of scope.)

## A2 — confirmed
- What I checked: The standard-Newton iterates for Case 1 (accnewtonaa.jl, x0=1.2, 12 iterations), where the tangent at x_n crosses zero, and the error ratios.
- Evidence: verify/check.py. The tangent's zero crossing is x_{n+1} by definition, and x_{n+1}-1 = e_n * (0.515, 0.508, 0.504, …, 0.500004). So every crossing lies on the same side as x_n, about half-way between x_n and x*=1. It is always closer to x* than x_n was, never "far from x*". Examples: 1.2→1.10303, 1.10303→1.05236, 1.05236→1.02640. committed/accnewtonaa/frame_004.png shows the blue tangents landing short of x_n, between it and 1, in a staircase that approaches 1 from the right. The page's own sentence just before it ("each step only halves the error") contradicts "crosses zero far from x*". The slowness comes from the step being too *short* (half the distance to the root), not from overshooting. "Nearly horizontal" also misleads: f'(x_n)≈6e_n goes to 0, but f(x_n)≈3e_n² goes to 0 faster, so the ratio gives a step of about e_n/2.
- Severity: Misleading is fair, arguably Error. As a statement about these iterates, "crosses zero far from x*" is simply false. It is not a defensible reading of the computed behavior. It does sit next to a correct statement (error halves), so Misleading is acceptable.
- Correction to the allegation: none.
