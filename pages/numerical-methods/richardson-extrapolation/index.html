Richardson Extrapolation

← Numerical Methods

Source inspiration: (Mathew 2000-2019).

Description

Richardson extrapolation improves an approximation that depends on a step size \(h\) by combining its values at two step sizes so that the leading term of its error cancels. Mathews applies it to numerical differentiation, where it turns the three-point central difference for \(f'(x)\) into a five-point formula; this page animates that step for \(f(x)=e^{-x}\sin(x)\) over \([0,\pi]\).

The three-point central difference is

\[ D_3(x,h)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}. \]

For smooth \(f\), Taylor expansion gives \(D_3(x,h)=f'(x)+\tfrac{f'''(x)}{6}h^2+\tfrac{f^{(5)}(x)}{120}h^4+\cdots\), with only even powers of \(h\). Halving \(h\) divides the \(h^2\) term by 4, so the combination

\[ D_5(x,h)=\frac{4D_3(x,h/2)-D_3(x,h)}{3}=\frac{f(x-h)-8f(x-h/2)+8f(x+h/2)-f(x+h)}{6h} \]

removes it. This is Mathews’ five-point central difference with spacing \(h/2\). In general, if \(A(h)=A+c\,h^p+O(h^q)\) with \(q>p\), then \(\bigl(2^pA(h/2)-A(h)\bigr)/(2^p-1)=A+O(h^q)\).

The method assumes that the exponent \(p\) of the leading error term is known and that this term dominates, which needs \(f\) smooth enough. As a general result, if \(f\in C^3[x-h,x+h]\) then \(f'(x)-D_3(x,h)=-\tfrac{f'''(c)}{6}h^2\), and if \(f\in C^5[x-h,x+h]\) then

\[ f'(x)-D_5(x,h)=\frac{f^{(5)}(c)}{480}\,h^4 \]

for some \(c\) in that interval, which is Mathews’ five-point remainder \(\tfrac{f^{(5)}(c)}{30}k^4\) with \(k=h/2\). So halving \(h\) divides the error of \(D_3\) by about 4 and that of \(D_5\) by about 16. In Case 1, going from \(h=0.02\) to \(h=0.01\) takes the maximum error over \([0,\pi]\) from \(1.33\times10^{-4}\) to \(3.33\times10^{-5}\) for \(D_3\) and from \(1.33\times10^{-9}\) to \(8.33\times10^{-11}\) for \(D_5\). In Case 2, at \(x=1\), the log-log slopes between successive step sizes are 2.000 to 2.007 for \(D_3\) and 3.905 to 4.005 for \(D_5\), from \(h=0.5\) down to \(h=0.006\).

Rounding error eventually wins. Both formulas subtract nearly equal values of \(f\) and divide by \(h\), so rounding errors grow as \(h\) shrinks while the truncation error of \(D_5\) falls like \(h^4\). The last two points of Case 2 leave the \(h^4\) line: at \(h=0.0015\) the computed error of \(D_5\) is \(1.29\times10^{-14}\), about 2.8 times the truncation error \(4.67\times10^{-15}\) found in 50-digit arithmetic. Wrong weights also mislead: a one-sided difference \(A(h)=\bigl(f(x+h)-f(x)\bigr)/h\) has an error starting with an \(h\) term, so the weights 4 and \(-1\) leave an \(O(h)\) error, and the right combination is \(2A(h/2)-A(h)\).

The difference formulas themselves are on Numerical Differentiation and Derive Numerical Differentiation Formulae, the \(O(h^p)\) notation is on Big “O” Truncation Error, Romberg Integration applies the same step repeatedly to the trapezoidal rule, and Aitken’s Method accelerates a sequence without a step size.

Behavior: Both approximations converge as \(h\) shrinks, but the Richardson curve tracks \(f'(x)\) much more closely because the dominant \(h^2\) error term is canceled.

Derivation Notes

If \(D_3(x,h)=f'(x)+C_2 h^2 + C_4 h^4 + \cdots\), then

\[ \frac{4D_3(x,h/2)-D_3(x,h)}{3} = f'(x) + O(h^4), \]

because the \(C_2 h^2\) contribution cancels exactly.
