Derive Numerical Differentiation Formulae

← Numerical Methods

Source inspiration: (Mathew 2000-2019).

Description

This page derives the central-difference formulas that approximate \(f'(x_0)\) and \(f''(x_0)\) from values of \(f\) at equally spaced points symmetric about \(x_0\). The legacy module derives the formulas for both derivatives together, in parallel, rather than treating each formula as an isolated trick. Each sample is replaced by its Taylor polynomial about \(x_0\),

\[ f(x_0+jh) \approx \sum_{k=0}^{n+2} \frac{(jh)^k}{k!}\, f^{(k)}(x_0), \]

with \(j=\pm1\) and \(n=2\) for three points, or \(j=\pm1,\pm2\) and \(n=4\) for five points (expansion degree 4 or 6, following Mathews). Treating \(f'(x_0),\dots,f^{(n)}(x_0)\) as the unknowns gives a small linear system, and one solve yields all the formulas. Two extra terms are needed because the first cancels from the \(f''\) formulas; keeping the lowest surviving term as the remainder, the solutions for the first two derivatives are, as Mathews states them,

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x_0) &= \frac{f(x_0+h)-f(x_0-h)}{2h} - \frac{h^2}{6}\, f^{(3)}(c), \\ f''(x_0) &= \frac{f(x_0+h)-2f(x_0)+f(x_0-h)}{h^2} - \frac{h^2}{12}\, f^{(4)}(c), \\ f'(x_0) &= \frac{f(x_0-2h)-8f(x_0-h)+8f(x_0+h)-f(x_0+2h)}{12h} + \frac{h^4}{30}\, f^{(5)}(c), \\ f''(x_0) &= \frac{-f(x_0-2h)+16f(x_0-h)-30f(x_0)+16f(x_0+h)-f(x_0+2h)}{12h^2} + \frac{h^4}{90}\, f^{(6)}(c). \end{aligned} \]

The formulas assume exact samples at equally spaced points. In general, each equation holds for some \(c\) between the outermost points it uses, provided the derivative in its remainder is continuous there, so \(f\) must be in \(C^3\), \(C^4\), \(C^5\) and \(C^6\) respectively. Because the points are symmetric about \(x_0\), each remainder contains only even powers of \(h\).

As a general result, the three-point formulas are \(O(h^2)\) and the five-point formulas \(O(h^4)\), so for small \(h\), when the derivative in the remainder is nonzero at \(x_0\), halving \(h\) divides their truncation errors by about 4 and 16. In Case 1, the error \(f'(1)\) minus the three-point slope, divided by \(h^2\), stays between \(-0.171\) and \(-0.169\), approaching \(-f^{(3)}(1)/6 \approx -0.1694\). In Case 2, from \(h=0.02\) to \(h=0.01\) the three-point and five-point errors fall by factors of \(4.00\) and \(15.99\).

More points do not always give a higher order: the five-point system also yields formulas for \(f'''(x_0)\) and \(f^{(4)}(x_0)\), and these are only \(O(h^2)\). Rounding also limits the formulas: each sample carries a rounding error of about \(\varepsilon|f|\), with \(\varepsilon \approx 2.2\times10^{-16}\), and the formulas for \(f'\) and \(f''\) divide it by \(h\) and \(h^2\), so for small enough \(h\) it outweighs the truncation error. Case 3 reaches that point: at \(h=0.005\) the largest five-point second-derivative error, \(5.5\times10^{-11}\), is almost all truncation error, but at \(h=0.002\) the largest truncation error is \(1.4\times10^{-12}\) while the largest computed error is \(5.9\times10^{-11}\).

Numerical Differentiation adds one-sided formulas and studies roundoff further, Richardson Extrapolation reaches the five-point first-derivative formula as \(\bigl(4D(h)-D(2h)\bigr)/3\) with \(D(h)\) the three-point formula, Big “O” Truncation Error explains the order notation, and Maclaurin and Taylor Series covers the expansions the derivation starts from.

Case 3 - second-derivative formulas on \([2h,\pi-2h]\)

Behavior: The curve profiles compare \(f''(x)\) against the three-point and five-point second-derivative stencils on \([2h,\pi-2h]\), where both stencils use only points in \([0,\pi]\); the maximum errors in each frame are taken over this window. Derivation Notes

From Taylor expansions about \(x\),

\[ f(x\pm h)=f(x)\pm hf'(x)+\frac{h^2}{2}f''(x)\pm\frac{h^3}{6}f^{(3)}(x)+\cdots \]

one obtains

\[ f'(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}+O(h^2), \quad f''(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}+O(h^2). \]

Including \(x\pm2h\) adds degrees of freedom and yields five-point formulas for \(f'\) and \(f''\) with \(O(h^4)\) truncation error for smooth \(f\) (the five-point formulas for \(f'''\) and \(f^{(4)}\) are only \(O(h^2)\)).
