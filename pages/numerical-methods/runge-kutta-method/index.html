Runge-Kutta Method

← Numerical Methods

Source inspiration: (Mathew 2000-2019).

Description

The Runge-Kutta method of order 4 (RK4) solves the initial value problem \(y' = f(t, y)\), \(y(t_0) = y_0\) over \([t_0, b]\). It computes approximations \(y_i \approx y(t_i)\) at the points \(t_i = t_0 + ih\), with \(m\) subintervals of width \(h = (b - t_0)/m\). Each step evaluates \(f\) four times, once at the start of the step, twice at its midpoint and once at its end, and advances by a weighted average of the four increments:

\[ \begin{aligned} k_1 &= h\,f(t_i,\ y_i), &\qquad k_2 &= h\,f\!\left(t_i + \tfrac{h}{2},\ y_i + \tfrac{k_1}{2}\right),\\ k_3 &= h\,f\!\left(t_i + \tfrac{h}{2},\ y_i + \tfrac{k_2}{2}\right), &\qquad k_4 &= h\,f(t_i + h,\ y_i + k_3), \end{aligned} \]

\[ y_{i+1} = y_i + \frac{k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4}{6}. \]

When \(f\) depends on \(t\) alone, \(k_2 = k_3\) and the step is Simpson’s rule on \([t_i, t_i + h]\), which explains the weights \(1, 2, 2, 1\).

Mathews’ theorem assumes that \(f\) is continuous and satisfies a Lipschitz condition in \(y\), so that the problem has a unique solution. The error result needs more: in general, if \(f\) has continuous partial derivatives through order 4 near the solution (which makes \(y \in C^5[t_0, b]\)), the local truncation error of a step is \(O(h^5)\) and the global error is \(|y(t_i) - y_i| = O(h^4)\), so for small enough \(h\) halving the step divides the final error by about 16.

Case 1 runs the sweep on \(f(t, y) = 1 - t\sqrt[3]{y}\), \(y(0) = 1\), over \([0, 5]\). The solution stays positive, down to \(y(5) = 0.0081199\), so \(f\) is smooth near it and the general rate applies, but only for steps smaller than the animation shows. The observed order, \(\log(e_{\text{prev}}/e)/\log(h_{\text{prev}}/h)\) for the error \(e\) in \(y(5)\), is still \(4.90\) between the last two frames, \(m = 120\) and \(150\), and \(4.07\) between \(m = 1200\) and \(1600\), runs far finer than any frame.

Large steps fail on this problem in two ways. Along the solution, \(\partial f/\partial y = -t/(3y^{2/3})\) reaches \(-41.3\) at \(t = 5\), so for every step longer than about \(h = 0.0675\) (\(m \le 74\)), \(h\,|\partial f/\partial y|\) exceeds RK4’s general linear stability limit of \(2.785\) before \(t = 5\). And \(f\) is not Lipschitz near \(y = 0\): in every run from \(m = 12\) to \(52\) some stage value falls below \(0\), where \(\sqrt[3]{y}\) has an infinite slope. In those same runs the computed curve visibly turns upward in the tail instead of decaying, ending at \(y(5) \approx 0.588\) for \(m = 12\); the case’s Behavior note separates the causes.

Euler’s method (order 1), Heun’s method (order 2) and the Taylor Series Method animate the same sweep on the same problem, Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg pairs embedded fourth- and fifth-order formulas to choose \(h\) adaptively, and Adams-Bashforth-Moulton takes its starting values from a one-step method such as RK4.

Animations

The animation below repeats Mathews’ step-refinement sweep for the IVP \(y' = 1 - t\sqrt[3]{y}\), \(y(0)=1\) over \([0,5]\). Each frame is a complete RK4 run with \(m\) subintervals (\(h=5/m\)), for the 21 values of Mathews’ animation, \(m = 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 52, 56, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 120, 150\), drawn over a fine-step reference solution and labelled with the computed \(y(5)\) and its error. RK4 computes four increments \(k_j = h\,f(\cdot)\) from slope estimates (\(k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4\)) per step and adds their weighted average \((k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4)/6\) to \(y_i\) — for smooth \(f\) this gives \(O(h^5)\) local truncation error and \(O(h^4)\) global error. This \(f\) is not smooth at \(y=0\), where \(\partial f/\partial y=-t/(3y^{2/3})\) is unbounded, and the coarser runs below evaluate it at negative \(y\).

Case 1 — RK4 step-refinement sweep, \(y' = 1 - t\sqrt[3]{y}\), \(y(0)=1\), \(m = 12\) to \(150\) subintervals

Behavior: The reference solution rises from \(y(0)=1\) to about \(1.4498\) near \(t=0.88\), then decays toward the nullcline \(y=1/t^3\) (where \(y'=0\)) and reaches \(y(5)=0.0081199\). For \(m=12\) through \(52\) the computed curve visibly turns upward before \(t=5\) instead of following the decay, ending at \(y(5)\approx0.588\) for \(m=12\), \(0.289\) for \(m=20\) and \(0.0377\) for \(m=52\); at \(m=56\) it still turns upward on the last step, but only by \(8.1\times10^{-4}\), too little to see, and ends at \(y(5)\approx0.0099\). The error in \(y(5)\) falls at every refinement in the sweep: \(3.4\times10^{-4}\) at \(m=60\), \(2.8\times10^{-5}\) at \(m=70\), \(6.8\times10^{-6}\) at \(m=80\) and \(1.8\times10^{-7}\) at \(m=150\), where \(y(5)\approx0.0081201\). The stability bound: along the reference solution \(\partial f/\partial y=-t/(3y^{2/3})\) reaches \(-41.3\) at \(t=5\), so for steps longer than about \(h=0.0675\) (\(m\le74\); here \(m=12\) to \(70\)), \(h\,|\partial f/\partial y|\) evaluated along the reference solution exceeds RK4’s linear stability limit \(2.785\) before \(t=5\) (the shaded region in those frames). Just above \(h=0.0675\) this affects only the last steps (\(m=70\): from \(t\approx4.91\)), but at \(m=12\) it starts at \(t\approx3.10\). For \(m=80\) to \(150\), \(h\,|\partial f/\partial y|\) along the reference stays below \(2.785\) up to \(t=5\). This bound concerns small departures from the reference, not the drawn run: for \(m=12\) to \(24\) the run’s own \(h\,|\partial f/\partial y|\) at its grid values stays below \(2.42\). What the runs do: for \(m=12\) to \(18\) the upturn begins with one long step that starts before the shaded region (\(t\approx2.92\) to \(3.33\) for \(m=12\)), and \(94\) to \(100\%\) of the error just after it (\(0.168\) for \(m=12\)) is what that step produces even when started from the exact \(y(t_i)\). For \(m=20\) to \(56\) the first upward step started from the exact \(y(t_i)\) would produce only \(0.3\) to \(20\%\) of the error after it; the rest comes from the error already present at \(t_i\), which RK4’s step map multiplies by \(2.9\) to \(18.6\), whereas the exact flow over that step would shrink it to \(2\) to \(6\%\) of its size. For \(m=60\) and \(70\) the bound is crossed but the run does not turn upward. The size of the upturns depends on how \(\sqrt[3]{y}\) is taken for \(y<0\). In every run from \(m=12\) to \(52\), and in no other, some RK4 stage value falls below \(0\) (as low as \(-1.15\)), first always \(y_i+k_3\), where \(k_4\) is evaluated: in the first upward step for \(m=12\) to \(24\) and \(36\), and in the step after it for \(m=28\), \(32\) and \(40\) to \(52\). The stage has then crossed \(y=0\), where \(\sqrt[3]{y}\) is not differentiable; the script takes the real cube root there, as Mathews’ printed \(y(5)\) values show he did, so \(f=1+t|y|^{1/3}>1\). With \(\sqrt[3]{\max(y,0)}\) instead, the summed upward steps after the peak shrink in each of these runs (from \(0.46\) to \(0\) for \(m=12\), from \(0.028\) to \(0.012\) for \(m=52\)), and \(y(5)\) becomes \(-0.239\) for \(m=12\), \(0.0054\) for \(m=20\) and \(0.021\) for \(m=52\). The \(m=56\) run has no negative stage, so its last-step rise is the same either way.
